CHAPTER THREE
METHODOLOGY
3.1 Introduction
This chapter presents the methodology adopted in the design, development, implementation and evaluation of the proposed AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System. The methodology provides a systematic framework that guided the development of the application from problem identification to system deployment. It explains the research design, system requirements, system design, development tools, implementation procedures, testing methods and ethical considerations employed throughout the study.
Artificial Intelligence has become an important technology in modern agriculture because of its ability to improve decision-making, increase productivity and reduce crop losses through early disease diagnosis. Recent advances in deep learning have significantly improved plant disease identification by enabling computer systems to automatically recognise complex disease patterns from digital images with high accuracy (Barbedo, 2022). Researchers have also demonstrated that convolutional neural networks outperform many traditional machine learning techniques in crop disease classification because they automatically learn hierarchical image features without requiring manual feature extraction (Haridasan, Thomas & Raj, 2022).
The proposed system integrates mobile application development, web application development and artificial intelligence into a single platform that enables farmers to capture crop leaf images using a smartphone and receive immediate disease diagnosis together with recommended treatment and prevention measures. The mobile application will be developed using Flutter because it supports cross-platform development while providing high performance and a consistent user experience across Android devices. The backend services will be developed using Laravel 12, which provides secure authentication, efficient routing, RESTful API support and robust database management features suitable for enterprise software applications. The system will use MySQL as its relational database management system to store user accounts, disease information, diagnosis history and treatment recommendations. The artificial intelligence component will be implemented using Python, TensorFlow and OpenCV to train and deploy a Convolutional Neural Network capable of recognising crop diseases from leaf images.
This research adopts the Design Science Research methodology because the primary objective is to develop and evaluate an innovative technological artefact capable of solving a practical agricultural problem. Design Science Research is widely recognised within information systems research for creating solutions that address real-world organisational challenges through systematic design and evaluation (Hevner et al., 2004). To complement this approach, the Agile Software Development Methodology was adopted because it promotes iterative development, continuous testing, stakeholder feedback and incremental software improvement throughout the development lifecycle (Sommerville, 2021).
The development process consists of several phases, including requirements analysis, system design, dataset collection, image pre-processing, artificial intelligence model development, backend development, mobile application development, system integration, testing and deployment. Each phase contributes towards producing a secure, reliable and user-friendly mobile application capable of assisting farmers in the early detection and management of crop diseases. Furthermore, the methodology ensures that the developed system satisfies both the functional and non-functional requirements identified during the problem analysis stage while providing a practical solution for improving agricultural productivity.
This chapter therefore provides a comprehensive description of the methodologies, technologies and procedures adopted during the development of the proposed AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System.
3.2 Research Design
Research design provides a systematic framework that guides the processes involved in achieving the objectives of a research study. It defines the procedures for collecting data, designing the proposed solution, implementing the system and evaluating its performance. An appropriate research design ensures that the research is conducted in a structured manner while producing reliable and valid findings (Creswell & Creswell, 2023).
This study adopted the Design Science Research (DSR) methodology integrated with the Agile Software Development Methodology. Design Science Research was selected because the primary objective of this study is to design, develop and evaluate an innovative software artefact capable of addressing the problem of delayed crop disease diagnosis among farmers. According to Hevner et al. (2004), Design Science Research is suitable for information systems research because it focuses on creating practical technological solutions that solve real-world problems through systematic design, implementation and evaluation. The methodology therefore provides an appropriate framework for developing an intelligent crop disease detection system that combines mobile computing and artificial intelligence technologies.
The Agile Software Development Methodology complements Design Science Research by supporting iterative software development, continuous testing and regular stakeholder feedback throughout the software development lifecycle. Sommerville (2021) states that Agile development encourages incremental software delivery, allowing developers to respond efficiently to changing user requirements while improving software quality after every iteration. This methodology was considered appropriate for the current study because the proposed system consists of several independent components, including the Flutter mobile application, Laravel backend, MySQL database and Python-based artificial intelligence model, all of which require continuous integration and testing throughout development.
The proposed research design also incorporates an experimental approach during the development of the artificial intelligence model. The experiment involves training, validating and testing a Convolutional Neural Network using labelled crop leaf images to determine its capability to classify healthy and diseased plants accurately. Experimental evaluation is widely adopted in deep learning research because it enables researchers to compare model performance using objective evaluation metrics such as accuracy, precision, recall and F1-score (Haridasan, Thomas & Raj, 2022). Furthermore, recent studies have shown that deep learning techniques have become the preferred approach for automated crop disease recognition because they eliminate the need for manual feature extraction while achieving higher classification accuracy than conventional machine learning algorithms (George et al., 2025; Khan et al., 2025).
The development of the proposed AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System was organised into several sequential but iterative phases. These phases include requirements analysis, system design, dataset collection, image pre-processing, artificial intelligence model development, backend development, mobile application development, system integration, testing and deployment. Although these phases are presented sequentially for ease of explanation, Agile development allowed the research to revisit earlier phases whenever improvements were identified during testing. This iterative approach reduced development risks and improved the overall quality of the final system.
3.2.1 Requirements Analysis
Requirements analysis is one of the most critical stages of software development because it establishes the foundation upon which the entire system is designed and implemented. During this phase, the functional and non-functional requirements of the proposed system were identified, analysed and documented to ensure that the final application satisfies the needs of its intended users. According to Sommerville (2021), requirements engineering enables developers to understand stakeholder expectations, minimise software development risks and produce systems that effectively solve the identified problem.
The requirements for the AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System were gathered through an extensive review of scholarly literature on crop disease detection, artificial intelligence in agriculture and mobile health technologies. Previous studies on plant disease recognition were analysed to identify the limitations of existing systems and determine features that could improve disease diagnosis and user experience. The literature revealed that most existing crop disease detection systems rely heavily on image classification using deep learning models, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), because of their superior capability to extract complex image features automatically (Barbedo, 2022). Similarly, Haridasan, Thomas and Raj (2022) observed that CNN-based approaches consistently outperform traditional machine learning algorithms in crop disease classification due to their ability to learn discriminative visual features directly from training images.
In addition to the literature review, the system requirements were derived from the objectives established in Chapter One and the practical challenges faced by farmers in diagnosing crop diseases. Many small-scale farmers experience delays in obtaining professional agricultural advice because agricultural extension officers are not always readily available. Consequently, diseases are often identified at advanced stages when crop damage has already occurred. Researchers have highlighted that delayed disease identification contributes significantly to reduced crop yields and food insecurity, particularly in developing countries where access to agricultural expertise is limited (Khan et al., 2025). These findings reinforced the need for an intelligent mobile application capable of providing immediate disease diagnosis using smartphone technology.
Based on the identified problem, the proposed system was designed to enable farmers to capture crop leaf images using a mobile device, submit the images for analysis, receive real-time disease predictions and obtain treatment and prevention recommendations. The system was also designed to maintain a diagnosis history, allowing users to review previous diagnoses and monitor disease occurrence over time. Furthermore, recognising that many farmers in Zimbabwe prefer communicating in local languages, the application incorporates a Shona voice assistant to improve accessibility and usability for users with limited digital literacy.
The requirements gathered during this phase were categorised into functional requirements and non-functional requirements. Functional requirements describe the specific services that the application must provide, whereas non-functional requirements specify the quality attributes that determine how effectively those services are delivered (Sommerville, 2021). These requirements guided the subsequent stages of system design, implementation and testing.
The requirements analysis phase therefore ensured that the proposed AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System was developed based on actual user needs rather than technological assumptions. By establishing clear system requirements before implementation, the research reduced the likelihood of design errors and ensured that the final application addresses the practical challenges associated with crop disease diagnosis.

Functional requirements define the specific operations that the proposed system must perform in order to satisfy user requirements. They describe the interactions between users and the application as well as the services provided by the software (Sommerville, 2021).

3.2.1.2 Non-Functional Requirements
Non-functional requirements describe the quality attributes that determine how effectively a software system performs its intended functions. Unlike functional requirements, which specify what the system should do, non-functional requirements define how well the system should perform under different operating conditions. These requirements are essential because they influence the reliability, usability, maintainability, security and overall performance of the developed system (Sommerville, 2021).
The AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System was designed to satisfy several non-functional requirements to ensure that it remains efficient, secure and user-friendly when deployed in real-world agricultural environments.
Performance
The system should process uploaded crop images and return disease diagnosis results within a reasonable period to minimise waiting time for farmers. Since timely disease identification is essential for reducing crop losses, the Artificial Intelligence model should produce predictions within a few seconds after image submission. TensorFlow-based deep learning models have demonstrated the ability to perform real-time image classification when deployed using optimised inference techniques (TensorFlow Developers, 2024).
Reliability
Reliability refers to the ability of the system to perform consistently without failure under normal operating conditions. The proposed application should consistently produce accurate disease predictions and remain available whenever users require its services. To improve reliability, the Laravel backend will implement robust error handling, exception management and input validation mechanisms, while the Artificial Intelligence model will be evaluated extensively before deployment to minimise incorrect predictions (Sommerville, 2021).
Security
Security is an important consideration because the application stores personal user information together with diagnosis records. The system therefore incorporates authentication and authorisation mechanisms to prevent unauthorised access to protected resources. Laravel provides built-in security features such as password hashing, authentication middleware, Cross-Site Request Forgery (CSRF) protection and SQL injection prevention, making it suitable for developing secure web services (Laravel Holdings, 2025). Furthermore, secure communication between the Flutter mobile application and the Laravel backend will be achieved through RESTful APIs protected using authentication tokens.
Usability
The proposed application is intended for use by farmers with varying levels of technological experience. Consequently, the user interface should remain simple, intuitive and easy to navigate. Large buttons, clear icons, readable typography and straightforward navigation structures will be implemented to improve usability. Research has shown that user-centred interface design significantly improves technology adoption among agricultural users, particularly older farmers who may possess limited digital literacy (Food and Agriculture Organization, 2022).
To further improve accessibility, the application incorporates a bilingual voice assistant supporting both English and Shona. The voice assistant enables users to receive disease diagnosis results and treatment recommendations through speech, reducing dependence on reading text displayed on the screen. This functionality is expected to improve accessibility for users with reading difficulties or limited formal education.
Scalability
The proposed system should accommodate future expansion without requiring significant architectural changes. Laravel supports modular software development, making it possible to introduce additional crop varieties, diseases and treatment recommendations as new agricultural information becomes available. Similarly, Flutter allows additional mobile features to be incorporated while maintaining a single codebase across supported platforms (Google, 2024).
Maintainability
Maintainability refers to the ease with which software can be modified, corrected or enhanced after deployment. The system adopts a modular architecture that separates the mobile application, backend services, database and Artificial Intelligence model into independent components. Such separation improves code readability, facilitates debugging and simplifies future software maintenance. According to Sommerville (2021), modular software architectures significantly reduce software maintenance costs because changes can be implemented without affecting unrelated components.
Availability
The application should remain available whenever users require crop disease diagnosis services. The backend server and database should therefore maintain high uptime to ensure uninterrupted service delivery. Database backup procedures and exception handling mechanisms will also be implemented to minimise service interruptions caused by hardware or software failures.
The non-functional requirements identified during this phase established the quality standards that guided the design and implementation of the proposed AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System. These requirements ensured that the final application not only fulfilled its intended functions but also achieved acceptable levels of performance, security, usability and reliability.

3.2.2 Data Collection and Dataset Preparation
The performance of any deep learning model depends significantly on the quality, diversity and quantity of the dataset used during training. Data collection and dataset preparation therefore constitute critical stages in the development of Artificial Intelligence systems because they directly influence the model's ability to learn meaningful patterns and produce accurate predictions. According to Barbedo (2022), the availability of high-quality and well-balanced datasets remains one of the most important factors affecting the performance of deep learning models in plant disease classification.
The dataset used in this study consists of labelled images of healthy and diseased crop leaves obtained from publicly available agricultural image repositories. The primary dataset was sourced from the PlantVillage dataset, which is one of the most widely used benchmark datasets for crop disease detection research. The PlantVillage dataset contains thousands of expertly labelled images covering multiple crop species and disease categories, making it suitable for training deep learning models for image classification (Hughes & Salathé, 2015). To improve the applicability of the proposed system to local agricultural conditions, additional crop disease images from reputable agricultural research publications and publicly available datasets were reviewed where appropriate.
The collected dataset included images representing both healthy and diseased leaves from commonly cultivated crops. Each image was assigned a corresponding class label indicating the crop species and disease category. Proper labelling was essential because supervised deep learning algorithms require correctly labelled training data to learn the relationship between image features and disease classes. Incorrectly labelled images can significantly reduce model accuracy and introduce bias during the training process (Haridasan, Thomas & Raj, 2022).
Before training the Convolutional Neural Network, the collected images underwent several pre-processing procedures to improve dataset quality and ensure compatibility with the selected deep learning architecture. Image pre-processing reduces noise, standardises image characteristics and improves the model's ability to identify discriminative features. Recent studies have demonstrated that appropriate image pre-processing significantly improves classification performance by reducing unnecessary variations within training datasets (Khan et al., 2025).
The first stage of dataset preparation involved removing duplicate, blurred and corrupted images that could negatively influence model learning. Images with poor lighting conditions, excessive background objects or incomplete leaf structures were also excluded to improve overall dataset quality. This ensured that the Artificial Intelligence model learned meaningful disease characteristics rather than irrelevant image artefacts.
Following dataset cleaning, all images were resized to a fixed resolution suitable for the Convolutional Neural Network architecture. Standardising image dimensions ensures that every image entering the neural network contains identical input dimensions, thereby reducing computational complexity and improving processing efficiency. TensorFlow documentation recommends maintaining consistent image dimensions throughout model training to optimise computational performance and improve convergence during learning (TensorFlow Developers, 2024).
Image normalisation was subsequently performed to scale pixel intensity values to a standard numerical range before training. Normalisation improves numerical stability during gradient optimisation and accelerates convergence of deep learning algorithms by reducing variation in pixel distributions across training samples (Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, 2016).
To improve the model's ability to generalise to previously unseen images, data augmentation techniques were applied to artificially increase dataset diversity. Data augmentation generated additional training samples through random image transformations such as horizontal flipping, slight rotations, zooming, brightness adjustment and translation. These transformations simulate natural variations that occur when farmers capture crop images using mobile devices under different environmental conditions. According to Shorten and Khoshgoftaar (2019), data augmentation reduces overfitting by exposing deep learning models to a broader range of image variations during training.
After pre-processing, the dataset was divided into three independent subsets: the training dataset, validation dataset and testing dataset. The training dataset was used to train the Convolutional Neural Network, the validation dataset was used to optimise model hyperparameters during training, while the testing dataset was reserved for evaluating the final performance of the trained model. A dataset split of 70% for training, 15% for validation and 15% for testing was adopted because it provides sufficient data for model learning while maintaining independent datasets for unbiased performance evaluation. Similar dataset partitioning strategies have been widely adopted in recent crop disease detection studies (Haridasan, Thomas & Raj, 2022).
The data collection and preparation process ensured that the Artificial Intelligence model was trained using high-quality, representative and well-prepared image data. This stage laid the foundation for developing a reliable deep learning model capable of accurately identifying crop diseases under practical agricultural conditions.

3.2.3 Image Pre-processing
Image pre-processing is an essential stage in the development of computer vision systems because it improves the quality of input images before they are presented to the deep learning model. The primary objective of image pre-processing is to reduce unwanted variations in image data, enhance important visual features and improve the overall accuracy of the classification model. According to Goodfellow, Bengio and Courville (2016), well-prepared input data enables neural networks to learn more representative features, resulting in improved prediction accuracy and faster model convergence during training.
Crop leaf images captured using mobile devices are often affected by variations in illumination, background complexity, image resolution and camera orientation. Such variations may reduce the performance of deep learning models if they are not addressed during data preparation. Consequently, several image pre-processing techniques were applied before training the Convolutional Neural Network to ensure that the dataset was consistent and suitable for machine learning. Similar approaches have been adopted by recent studies on plant disease detection using deep learning (Barbedo, 2022).
The first pre-processing operation involved resizing all images to a fixed resolution compatible with the selected Convolutional Neural Network architecture. Deep learning models require input images with uniform dimensions to ensure consistent feature extraction and efficient computation during training. In this study, each image was resized to 224 × 224 pixels, which is the standard input size used by many modern convolutional neural network architectures, including MobileNetV2, EfficientNet and ResNet (TensorFlow Developers, 2024).
Following image resizing, image normalisation was performed to standardise pixel intensity values before model training. Pixel values originally ranging from 0 to 255 were scaled to values between 0 and 1. Normalisation reduces numerical instability during gradient optimisation and accelerates the learning process by ensuring that all input features have comparable numerical ranges (Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, 2016).
Data augmentation techniques were then applied to increase the diversity of the training dataset and minimise overfitting. Data augmentation artificially generates additional training images through controlled image transformations while preserving the original disease characteristics. These transformations simulate real-world conditions under which farmers may capture crop images using mobile devices. According to Shorten and Khoshgoftaar (2019), data augmentation improves the ability of deep learning models to generalise to previously unseen images and significantly enhances classification performance.
The augmentation techniques applied during this study included horizontal flipping, random rotation, zooming, brightness adjustment and slight image translation. Horizontal flipping enables the model to recognise disease symptoms regardless of leaf orientation, while random rotation improves robustness to different camera angles. Brightness adjustment compensates for varying lighting conditions encountered in outdoor farming environments, whereas zooming and translation improve the model's ability to identify disease symptoms at different image scales and positions.
OpenCV was utilised to perform several image manipulation operations during the pre-processing stage. OpenCV provides an extensive collection of image processing functions that facilitate image resizing, colour space conversion and noise reduction while maintaining computational efficiency (OpenCV Team, 2024). TensorFlow was subsequently used to generate augmented image batches during model training, allowing new image variations to be created dynamically without permanently increasing dataset size.
Following augmentation, the dataset was partitioned into three independent subsets comprising the training dataset, validation dataset and testing dataset. Approximately 70% of the images were allocated for model training, 15% for validation and 15% for final model testing. The training dataset enabled the Convolutional Neural Network to learn disease features, the validation dataset was used to optimise hyperparameters and monitor model performance during training, while the testing dataset provided an unbiased evaluation of the final trained model. This dataset partitioning strategy has been widely adopted in recent deep learning studies because it balances learning efficiency with reliable performance evaluation (Haridasan, Thomas & Raj, 2022).
The image pre-processing stage significantly improved the quality and consistency of the dataset used during model development. By reducing image variability and increasing dataset diversity through augmentation, the proposed system was better equipped to learn robust disease characteristics capable of supporting accurate crop disease diagnosis under practical farming conditions.

3.2.4 Selection of the Deep Learning Model
The selection of an appropriate deep learning model is one of the most important decisions in the development of an Artificial Intelligence-based image classification system. Different Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) architectures possess varying levels of computational complexity, prediction accuracy and memory requirements. Consequently, selecting a suitable model requires balancing classification performance with computational efficiency, particularly when the final application is intended to operate on mobile devices with limited processing power.
Several state-of-the-art CNN architectures have been successfully applied in crop disease detection, including AlexNet, VGG16, ResNet50, EfficientNet and MobileNetV2. These architectures have consistently demonstrated excellent performance in image classification tasks due to their ability to automatically learn hierarchical visual features directly from raw images (Barbedo, 2022). However, although deeper architectures generally achieve high prediction accuracy, they often require substantial computational resources, making them less suitable for mobile applications where memory consumption and inference speed are critical considerations (Tan & Le, 2019).
MobileNetV2 was selected as the primary deep learning architecture for this study because it was specifically designed for mobile and embedded applications. Unlike conventional CNN architectures that perform standard convolution operations throughout the network, MobileNetV2 employs depthwise separable convolutions and inverted residual blocks, significantly reducing the number of model parameters and computational operations without substantially compromising classification accuracy (Sandler et al., 2018). This lightweight architecture enables faster inference while consuming less memory, making it particularly suitable for deployment within smartphone-based agricultural applications.
Another important consideration influencing the selection of MobileNetV2 was its compatibility with transfer learning. Transfer learning allows models that have already been trained on large image datasets, such as ImageNet, to be adapted to new classification tasks using comparatively smaller datasets. This approach reduces training time, improves model generalisation and minimises the amount of labelled data required to achieve satisfactory performance. Recent studies have demonstrated that transfer learning significantly improves plant disease classification accuracy while reducing computational costs associated with training deep neural networks from scratch (Barbedo, 2022; Haridasan, Thomas & Raj, 2022).
In addition to computational efficiency, MobileNetV2 offers high scalability and ease of deployment within TensorFlow Lite, making it appropriate for future deployment directly on mobile devices should offline disease diagnosis be required. Although the initial implementation of the proposed system performs disease classification through communication between the Flutter mobile application, Laravel backend and Python-based Artificial Intelligence service, selecting a lightweight architecture provides flexibility for future system enhancements without requiring substantial architectural modifications.
To ensure that the selected model satisfied the objectives of the study, its performance was evaluated using several machine learning evaluation metrics, including classification accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score and confusion matrix analysis. These performance indicators provide objective measures of the model's capability to distinguish healthy crops from diseased crops while identifying individual disease categories correctly. According to Powers (2020), using multiple evaluation metrics provides a more comprehensive assessment of classifier performance than relying solely on classification accuracy, particularly when datasets contain class imbalances.
The selection of MobileNetV2 therefore provides an appropriate balance between computational efficiency, prediction accuracy and deployment feasibility, making it suitable for the development of a mobile-based crop disease detection system intended for practical agricultural environments.

3.2.5 Artificial Intelligence Model Development
The Artificial Intelligence component of the proposed system is responsible for analysing crop leaf images and predicting the presence of diseases based on visual symptoms. This component forms the core of the AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System because it enables automatic disease identification without requiring direct intervention from agricultural specialists. The model will be developed using Python because of its extensive support for machine learning and computer vision libraries, including TensorFlow, Keras and OpenCV, which have become standard tools for deep learning research and application development (TensorFlow Developers, 2024).
The development of the deep learning model will follow a supervised learning approach. Supervised learning involves training a model using labelled data, where each training image is associated with its corresponding disease category. During training, the Convolutional Neural Network will learn to recognise distinctive visual characteristics associated with healthy and diseased crop leaves by adjusting its internal parameters to minimise prediction errors. According to Goodfellow, Bengio and Courville (2016), supervised learning enables deep neural networks to identify complex feature representations that improve classification performance when sufficient training data are available.
Transfer learning will be employed during model development to improve classification performance while reducing computational requirements. Instead of training a deep neural network from scratch, the selected MobileNetV2 architecture will utilise weights pre-trained on the ImageNet dataset. These pre-trained weights contain general image features learned from millions of images, enabling the model to adapt more efficiently to crop disease classification using comparatively fewer agricultural training samples. Previous studies have demonstrated that transfer learning improves classification accuracy while reducing training time in agricultural image recognition applications (Barbedo, 2022; Haridasan, Thomas & Raj, 2022).
Before training, the final classification layer of the MobileNetV2 model will be modified to correspond with the number of disease classes included in the prepared dataset. Earlier layers responsible for extracting general image features may initially remain frozen while the newly added classification layers are trained. After satisfactory initial performance has been achieved, selected convolutional layers may be fine-tuned to improve the model's ability to recognise disease-specific visual characteristics. This staged training strategy reduces the likelihood of overfitting while allowing the model to adapt to the characteristics of crop disease images (TensorFlow Developers, 2024).
The model will be trained using mini-batch gradient descent with the Adam optimisation algorithm. The Adam optimiser is widely adopted in deep learning because it combines the advantages of adaptive learning rate optimisation with efficient convergence during model training (Kingma & Ba, 2015). Categorical Cross-Entropy will be used as the loss function because the proposed system performs multi-class image classification, where each input image belongs to one disease category. Throughout the training process, the model's performance will be monitored using the validation dataset to detect overfitting and determine the optimal number of training epochs.
To further improve the robustness of the model, regularisation techniques such as dropout and early stopping will be incorporated during training. Dropout reduces overfitting by randomly deactivating selected neurons during each training iteration, thereby encouraging the network to learn more generalised feature representations. Early stopping monitors validation performance and automatically terminates training when additional epochs no longer improve model performance. These techniques have been shown to improve the generalisation capability of deep learning models across various image classification tasks (Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, 2016).
Following training, the completed model will be exported in TensorFlow format and integrated into a Python-based inference service responsible for processing prediction requests received from the Laravel backend. When a farmer uploads a crop leaf image through the Flutter mobile application, the Laravel backend will securely transmit the image to the Artificial Intelligence service. The trained model will analyse the image, identify the most probable disease category and return the prediction together with a confidence score. Laravel will then retrieve the appropriate treatment and prevention recommendations from the MySQL database before transmitting the complete diagnosis back to the Flutter application through the RESTful API.
The development approach adopted for the Artificial Intelligence component ensures that the model remains modular and independent from the backend application. This modular architecture improves maintainability because future improvements to the deep learning model can be deployed without requiring significant modifications to the Flutter application or Laravel backend. Furthermore, separating the Artificial Intelligence service from the main application allows the system to accommodate newer deep learning architectures as advances in agricultural image recognition continue to emerge.

3.2.6 System Architecture and Integration Strategy
The proposed AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System adopts a distributed three-tier architecture comprising the presentation layer, application layer and data layer. This architectural approach separates the user interface, business logic and data management into independent components, thereby improving system scalability, maintainability and security. According to Sommerville (2021), layered software architectures promote modular system development by reducing dependencies between software components and simplifying future maintenance.
The presentation layer consists of the Flutter mobile application, which serves as the primary interface through which farmers interact with the system. Flutter was selected because it enables the development of high-performance cross-platform mobile applications using a single codebase while maintaining a consistent user experience across supported devices (Google, 2024). Through the mobile application, users will register accounts, authenticate themselves, capture crop leaf images using the smartphone camera, submit images for analysis and receive disease diagnosis results together with treatment and prevention recommendations.
The application layer consists of two independent but interconnected services: the Laravel backend and the Python-based Artificial Intelligence service. Laravel serves as the central application server responsible for authenticating users, validating requests, managing business logic, communicating with the MySQL database and exposing secure RESTful APIs that facilitate communication between the mobile application and the Artificial Intelligence component. Laravel was selected because of its robust Model-View-Controller (MVC) architecture, built-in authentication mechanisms, middleware support and efficient database management capabilities, which simplify the development of secure and maintainable web applications (Laravel Holdings, 2025).
The Artificial Intelligence service operates independently of the Laravel backend and is implemented using Python, TensorFlow and OpenCV. This service is responsible for receiving crop leaf images from Laravel, performing image pre-processing, executing disease classification using the trained MobileNetV2 model and returning prediction results to the backend application. Separating the Artificial Intelligence component from the main backend improves modularity because updates to the machine learning model can be deployed without affecting other system components. Such modular software architectures improve maintainability and facilitate future system enhancements (Sommerville, 2021).
The data layer is implemented using MySQL, which stores all persistent application data, including user accounts, crop information, disease descriptions, treatment recommendations and diagnosis history. MySQL was selected because it provides reliable relational data management, supports structured query processing and integrates efficiently with Laravel through the Eloquent Object Relational Mapping (ORM) framework (Oracle Corporation, 2024). The database also maintains relationships between application entities, thereby ensuring data integrity and reducing redundancy.
Communication between the various system components is achieved through RESTful Application Programming Interfaces (APIs). When a farmer captures and uploads a crop image using the Flutter mobile application, the image is transmitted securely to the Laravel backend through an HTTP request. Laravel authenticates the request, validates the uploaded image and forwards it to the Python Artificial Intelligence service for analysis. After completing disease classification, the Artificial Intelligence service returns the predicted disease category together with a confidence score to Laravel. The backend subsequently retrieves the corresponding treatment and prevention information from the MySQL database before returning a structured JSON response to the Flutter application. The mobile application then displays the diagnosis results to the user in both textual and voice formats.
The integration strategy adopted in this study enables each system component to perform specialised functions while remaining loosely coupled with other components. This approach improves software maintainability, simplifies debugging and allows future enhancements to be implemented independently. For example, improvements to the Artificial Intelligence model can be deployed without modifying the mobile application, while additional mobile features can be introduced without requiring changes to the machine learning service. Such flexibility is one of the major advantages of service-oriented software architectures used in modern intelligent applications (Richardson & Smith, 2022).

3.2.7 System Workflow
The system workflow describes the sequence of processes performed by the AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System from the moment a user interacts with the mobile application until a disease diagnosis is returned. Defining the workflow provides a clear understanding of how the various system components interact to deliver the required functionality. According to Sommerville (2021), modelling system workflows before implementation improves software design by identifying the interactions between users, software components and external services.
The workflow begins when a registered user launches the Flutter mobile application and authenticates using valid login credentials. Authentication is handled by the Laravel backend, which verifies the user's credentials against records stored in the MySQL database. Successful authentication grants the user access to the application's dashboard, while unsuccessful authentication results in an appropriate error message prompting the user to provide valid credentials. Secure authentication is essential for protecting sensitive user information and preventing unauthorised access to the application (Laravel Holdings, 2025).
After successful authentication, the farmer navigates to the disease detection module and captures a photograph of a crop leaf using the smartphone camera or selects an existing image from the device gallery. Before transmission, the mobile application validates the selected image to ensure that it is in a supported image format and satisfies the required size limitations. Performing basic validation on the client side reduces unnecessary communication with the server and improves the overall responsiveness of the application (Google, 2024).
The validated image is then transmitted to the Laravel backend through a secure RESTful API request. Upon receiving the image, Laravel performs additional validation checks to verify file integrity and confirm that the uploaded file satisfies the predefined validation rules. Once validation has been completed successfully, the backend temporarily stores the uploaded image and forwards it to the Python-based Artificial Intelligence service for analysis.
The Artificial Intelligence service receives the uploaded image and performs the image pre-processing operations described in the previous section. These operations include image resizing, normalisation and any additional transformations required by the trained MobileNetV2 model. The pre-processed image is subsequently passed through the trained Convolutional Neural Network, which analyses the visual characteristics of the leaf and predicts the most probable disease category together with a confidence score indicating the certainty of the prediction. Recent studies have shown that combining convolutional neural networks with appropriate image pre-processing techniques significantly improves disease classification performance in agricultural applications (Barbedo, 2022; Haridasan, Thomas & Raj, 2022).
After completing the prediction process, the Artificial Intelligence service returns the disease name and confidence score to the Laravel backend. Laravel then queries the MySQL database to retrieve additional information associated with the predicted disease, including a description of the disease, possible causes, recommended treatment methods and preventive measures. The diagnosis record is subsequently stored within the user's diagnosis history, allowing previous predictions to be retrieved for future reference.
Once all required information has been assembled, Laravel constructs a structured JSON response and transmits it back to the Flutter mobile application. The application displays the disease diagnosis, confidence score, treatment recommendations and preventive measures using an intuitive graphical interface. Where required, the integrated voice assistant converts the diagnosis and recommendations into spoken output in either English or Shona, thereby improving accessibility for users who may experience difficulties reading on-screen information.
If the Artificial Intelligence model is unable to classify the uploaded image with sufficient confidence or if the uploaded image does not meet the required quality standards, the application notifies the user and requests another image. This process reduces the likelihood of incorrect diagnoses while encouraging users to capture clearer images under suitable lighting conditions.
The workflow adopted in this study ensures efficient communication between the Flutter mobile application, Laravel backend, Python Artificial Intelligence service and MySQL database. By clearly defining the responsibilities of each software component, the proposed architecture improves maintainability, scalability and overall system reliability while providing farmers with a seamless disease diagnosis experience.

The proposed AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System consists of multiple integrated components, including the Flutter mobile application, Laravel backend, MySQL database and Python-based Artificial Intelligence service. Since each component performs specialised functions, a comprehensive testing strategy was adopted to ensure that individual modules function correctly before the integrated system is evaluated. The testing process therefore consists of unit testing, integration testing, system testing, user acceptance testing, performance testing and Artificial Intelligence model evaluation.
3.7.1 Unit Testing
Unit testing was conducted to verify that individual software modules function correctly before integration with other system components. Each module was tested independently to ensure that it produced the expected output under different operating conditions. Sommerville (2021) states that unit testing simplifies software debugging by isolating individual components and identifying defects before complete system integration.
For the Flutter mobile application, unit testing focused on user interface components, form validation, authentication screens and image upload functionality. Individual widgets were tested to ensure that user interactions produced the expected behaviour.
Within the Laravel backend, unit testing was performed on controllers, models, middleware and API endpoints. Each controller method was evaluated independently to verify that user requests were processed correctly and that appropriate responses were returned.
The Artificial Intelligence component was also subjected to unit testing by evaluating individual functions responsible for image pre-processing, dataset loading, model inference and prediction generation. This ensured that each processing stage operated correctly before integration into the complete prediction pipeline.

3.7.2 Integration Testing
Integration testing was performed after combining the Flutter mobile application, Laravel backend, Artificial Intelligence service and MySQL database into a single operational system. The objective of integration testing was to verify that information flowed correctly between interconnected system components without communication failures or data inconsistencies.
During this phase, API communication between Flutter and Laravel was evaluated to ensure that user requests were transmitted correctly and appropriate JSON responses were returned. Communication between Laravel and the Python Artificial Intelligence service was also verified to ensure that uploaded crop images were successfully analysed and disease predictions returned accurately. Database operations were tested to confirm that diagnosis records were correctly stored and retrieved from MySQL.

3.7.3 System Testing
System testing was conducted after completing software integration to determine whether the complete application satisfied the functional and non-functional requirements identified during system analysis. Unlike unit testing and integration testing, system testing evaluates the application as a complete software product operating under realistic conditions.
The testing process evaluated all major application functions, including user registration, authentication, image upload, disease prediction, treatment recommendation, diagnosis history and administrator operations. Each function was executed using realistic user scenarios to verify that the application performed as expected without unexpected software failures.
System testing also evaluated navigation between application screens, response time, data consistency and error handling mechanisms. Successful completion of this phase demonstrated that the proposed system satisfied the requirements established during system analysis.
3.7.4 Artificial Intelligence Model Evaluation
Unlike conventional software systems, Artificial Intelligence applications require additional evaluation to determine the predictive performance of the trained model. Consequently, the proposed Convolutional Neural Network will be evaluated using several widely accepted machine learning performance metrics.
The primary evaluation metric will be classification accuracy, which measures the proportion of correctly classified crop leaf images relative to the total number of testing images. Although accuracy provides an overall indication of model performance, it may produce misleading results when datasets contain imbalanced classes (Powers, 2020). Therefore, additional evaluation metrics will also be utilised.
Precision will be calculated to determine the proportion of disease predictions that are correctly classified, while recall will evaluate the model's ability to identify all diseased crops correctly. The F1-score will subsequently combine precision and recall into a single performance measure, providing a balanced assessment of model effectiveness.
A confusion matrix will also be generated to provide a detailed visual representation of the classification performance for each disease category. The confusion matrix enables identification of disease classes that are frequently misclassified, thereby supporting future improvements to the model (Goodfellow, Bengio & Courville, 2016).
3.7.5 Performance Testing
Performance testing was conducted to evaluate the responsiveness and efficiency of the proposed system under normal operating conditions. This phase measured image upload speed, API response time, database query performance and Artificial Intelligence prediction time.
Performance testing also assessed memory consumption and processor utilisation during simultaneous execution of the Flutter application, Laravel backend and Artificial Intelligence service. Maintaining acceptable response times is essential because delays in disease diagnosis may reduce user confidence in the application.
The expected system response time for disease prediction is less than five seconds under normal network conditions.

3.8 Ethical and Safety Considerations
Ethical and safety considerations are fundamental in the development and deployment of software systems, particularly those that process user information and utilise Artificial Intelligence to support decision-making. These considerations ensure that the developed system operates responsibly, protects user privacy and complies with accepted ethical principles governing software development. According to the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM, 2018), software developers have a professional responsibility to design systems that are secure, transparent, reliable and beneficial to society.
The proposed AI-Powered Mobile Crop Disease Detection System processes personal information submitted by registered users together with images of crops captured using mobile devices. Although crop images generally do not contain highly sensitive personal information, user registration details, diagnosis history and uploaded images constitute personal data that must be protected against unauthorised access. Consequently, appropriate security mechanisms will be implemented to safeguard user information throughout the system.
3.8.1 Privacy and Confidentiality
The system will collect only the information necessary for user registration, authentication and disease diagnosis. Personal information including names, email addresses, telephone numbers and diagnosis records will be stored securely within the MySQL database. Access to this information will be restricted to authorised users and system administrators through authentication and role-based access control.
Password credentials will never be stored in plain text. Instead, Laravel's password hashing mechanisms will be utilised to encrypt passwords before storage, thereby reducing the risk of unauthorised access should the database become compromised. Laravel's built-in authentication framework provides secure password management and protection against common security vulnerabilities such as SQL injection and Cross-Site Request Forgery attacks (Laravel Holdings, 2025).
In addition, communication between the Flutter mobile application and the Laravel backend should be secured using Hypertext Transfer Protocol Secure (HTTPS). Encrypting data transmitted across the network reduces the risk of interception by malicious third parties and improves the overall security of the application (OWASP Foundation, 2023).
3.8.2 Responsible Use of Artificial Intelligence
Artificial Intelligence technologies should be used responsibly because incorrect predictions may influence users' decisions regarding crop management. Although the proposed system is designed to provide accurate disease diagnosis, it should not replace professional agricultural advice. Instead, the application should function as a decision-support tool that assists farmers in identifying possible crop diseases before seeking confirmation from qualified agricultural extension officers where necessary.
Researchers have emphasised that Artificial Intelligence systems should remain transparent regarding their limitations to prevent users from placing excessive confidence in automated predictions (European Commission, 2021). Consequently, the proposed application will display a confidence score together with every prediction to indicate the certainty associated with the diagnosis. Where prediction confidence is low, users will be encouraged to capture another image or consult agricultural professionals before implementing treatment recommendations.
3.8.3 Dataset Integrity and Bias
The quality of an Artificial Intelligence system depends largely upon the quality and representativeness of the dataset used during model training. Training datasets that are biased or unrepresentative may produce discriminatory or inaccurate predictions when applied in practical environments. Therefore, efforts will be made to utilise a diverse dataset containing healthy and diseased crop images captured under varying environmental conditions.
The dataset will be reviewed to remove duplicate, corrupted and incorrectly labelled images before training the deep learning model. Furthermore, data augmentation techniques will be employed to improve dataset diversity and reduce the likelihood of model overfitting. According to Barbedo (2022), improving dataset quality significantly enhances the robustness and generalisation capability of deep learning models developed for plant disease detection.
3.8.4 System Security
Software security is an essential consideration because the proposed application communicates through internet-based services. Appropriate security controls will therefore be implemented throughout the system to minimise potential cyber security threats.
Laravel provides numerous built-in security features, including authentication middleware, input validation, encrypted password storage, protection against SQL injection attacks and Cross-Site Request Forgery protection (Laravel Holdings, 2025). These features will be utilised to secure backend operations and protect user information.
Similarly, uploaded crop images will undergo server-side validation before being processed by the Artificial Intelligence service. Restricting accepted file formats and limiting upload sizes reduces the likelihood of malicious file uploads while improving overall system reliability.
3.8.5 Accessibility and Inclusivity
One of the principal objectives of the proposed system is to improve access to crop disease diagnosis for farmers with varying levels of technological experience. Consequently, accessibility considerations have influenced both the design and implementation of the mobile application.
The user interface will incorporate large interactive buttons, readable typography and simple navigation structures suitable for elderly users and individuals with limited digital literacy. Furthermore, the application will integrate a bilingual voice assistant capable of communicating diagnosis results and treatment recommendations in both English and Shona. This functionality improves accessibility for users who experience reading difficulties or who prefer receiving agricultural information in their local language.
Designing inclusive software applications promotes wider technology adoption and contributes towards reducing the digital divide within rural communities (World Bank, 2021).
3.8.6 Ethical Approval
This study will be conducted in accordance with the ethical guidelines established by the institution under which the research is undertaken. Any data collected during system testing involving human participants will be obtained only after participants have been informed of the purpose of the study and have voluntarily agreed to participate.
Participants involved in User Acceptance Testing will retain the right to withdraw from the study at any stage without penalty. Information collected during testing will be used solely for academic purposes and will not be disclosed to unauthorised third parties.
